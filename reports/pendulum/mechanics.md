# Mechanics-informed reference

The primary NeuralSVD-versus-classical comparison does not assume knowledge of the equations of motion. This additional reference is given a damped pendulum force law, the semi-implicit update scheme and the calibration of the first three sensor channels. It is therefore a privileged comparator. It estimates three unknown quantities from the same training paths: restoring coefficient a, damping response b, and additive velocity diffusion σ. It never uses target transitions or target conditional means during fitting. It is inapplicable to the partial sensor map because angular velocity cannot be decoded there.

From the full observations reconstruct θ modulo 2π by atan2(sin θ,cos θ), and ω by 2 atanh(v). Each training macro interval contains eight equal, hidden substeps. At substep size d the fitted law sets ω′ = (1−bdγ)ω−ad sin θ + σ√d ξ and θ′ = θ + dω′. Fit (a,b) by constrained nonlinear least squares between observed final macro-interval velocities and the integrated noise-free drift; estimate σ by matching the mean squared macro-step innovation with its predicted variance. This is a small-noise quasi-fit, not a claim of unbiased nonlinear system identification.

Prediction propagates the mean and the three distinct covariance entries of the unwrapped angle and velocity. Under a Gaussian input law for (θ,ω), E sin θ = exp(−Var(θ)/2) sin Eθ and Cov(ω,sin θ) = Cov(ω,θ) E cos θ. Together with the second trigonometric moment, these identities give the exact first two moments of each individual substep for Gaussian input. Approximating each subsequent non-Gaussian output by its moment-matched Gaussian is the closure assumption. The bounded forecast is exp(−Var(θ)/2) cos Eθ. It is not an exact conditional mean for general nonlinear pendulum laws. No Monte Carlo simulation occurs during prediction.

**Extra diagnostic fixed before running it.** With the primary fits (seeds 71–75), evaluate the same two held-out schedules at horizon six, at 128 new full-state starts with 1,024 independent simulator continuations per start. Compare the fit with the very same fitted drift but zero process diffusion during inference. Record signed bias-corrected MSE, its independent-start standard error, and numerical reference variance in [the raw audit](mechanics.json). Report RMSE after averaging the schedules' signed MSE within a fit, never by independently clamping the two schedules' MSEs. No model is selected on this comparison. Absolute MSE near zero can be below Monte Carlo resolution.

Reproduce from the repository root: `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python experiments/pendulum_mechanics.py`. The main study separately evaluates this reference on its matched test/calibration paths.

On this fresh audit the mean sixth-horizon RMSE across five independent fits was **0.00526** for the stochastic moment model and **0.02337** for its zero-diffusion ablation. The schedule-averaged stochastic MSE was positive for each fit (range 0.0000050–0.0000968), but some individual values approach the independent-start uncertainty; do not interpret the last decimal as physical accuracy. Across fits the estimated coefficients (mean ± population SD) were a = 0.9938 ± 0.0135, b = 1.0007 ± 0.0052, and σ = 0.3608 ± 0.0026. The simulator coefficients (1, 1, 0.36) are used only for this after-fit check. Maps A and B give identical forecasts for matched latent starts because their calibrated physical channels are identical. The small errors are evidence about this correctly specified force/sensor family; they do not establish that the reference transfers to unknown equations or sensors.
